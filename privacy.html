Privacy policy

Effective date: 17 November 2026 (earlier versions: 15 November 2026, YouTube only; Instagram and Facebook added earlier on 17 November)

This policy explains what Ari Uploader ("the app") accesses, collects, stores, uses and shares, and how you can control it. The app is a private tool run by one person (the "owner") to upload their own videos to their own YouTube channels and, where the owner has connected them, to their own TikTok accounts, Instagram accounts and Facebook Pages. It is not offered to the public.

1. YouTube API Services

The app uses the YouTube API Services. By using the app, you agree to be bound by the YouTube Terms of Service. Google's handling of your information is described in the Google Privacy Policy.

2. Google permissions the app asks for

When a channel is connected, Google's consent screen asks for two permissions:

  • Manage your YouTube videos (youtube.upload): to upload a video to the connected channel.
  • View your YouTube account (youtube.readonly): used only to read the connected channel's ID, name and handle, so the app can confirm the right channel was connected.

The app does not read your comments, subscribers, private videos, watch history, messages, payment details, or any Google data outside YouTube.

3. Information the app accesses and collects

  • Sign-in tokens that Google issues when you allow access (an OAuth refresh token per connected channel).
  • Channel details: the channel's ID, name and handle, and the date it was connected.
  • Upload records: for each video the owner chose to upload, its file name, title, description, tags, privacy setting, the date, and the video ID and link YouTube returns.

The app does not collect names, email addresses, phone numbers, contacts, location or any information about people who watch the videos.

4. How the information is used

  • To upload the owner's videos to the owner's chosen channel, after the owner has approved each upload.
  • To confirm which channel a token belongs to, so a video never goes to the wrong channel.
  • To keep a record of what was uploaded and when, so the same video is not uploaded twice.

The information is not used for advertising, is not sold, and is not used to train machine-learning or artificial-intelligence models.

5. Google API Services User Data Policy

The app's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

6. Instagram and Facebook (Meta Platform)

The app can also publish the owner's own finished videos as Instagram Reels, and as Facebook Page Reels, through Meta's official Graph API. It is used only with Instagram Business or Creator accounts, and Facebook Pages, that the owner owns or administers. No one else's account is connected, and the app does not offer sign-in to the public.

Instead of a public sign-in screen, the owner creates an access token for their own account in their own Meta developer app (for example a system user token in Meta Business Suite) and stores it in the app. The permissions the app uses:

  • instagram_basic: to read the connected Instagram account's ID and username, so the app can confirm the right account was connected, and to read the account's most recent posts, so it can confirm a post went up.
  • instagram_content_publish: to publish a video as a Reel to the connected Instagram account.
  • pages_show_list and pages_read_engagement: to find the Facebook Page linked to the Instagram account and read its name.
  • business_management: to reach the Page and Instagram account through the owner's Business.
  • pages_manage_posts: to publish a video as a Reel to the owner's Facebook Page.

The app does not read direct messages, comments, followers, audience details, insights about other people, or any Meta data beyond what is listed here. Its use of Meta data follows the Meta Platform Terms and the Meta Developer Policies. Meta's handling of your information is described in the Instagram Privacy Policy and the Meta Privacy Policy.

What the app stores for Instagram and Facebook: the access token (in the operating system's keychain, never in a plain file), the account's ID, username or Page name, and a record of each video the owner chose to publish (its file name, caption, date, and the post ID and link Meta returns). It does not collect personal information about people who watch the videos or who follow the account. It is not used for advertising, is not sold, is not shared with anyone except Meta in order to publish, and is not used to train machine-learning or artificial-intelligence models.

The video file and caption are sent to Meta's servers when the owner publishes, to create the post. The app has no server of its own and keeps no copy of that data outside the owner's computer.

7. TikTok

The app can also send the owner's own finished videos to the owner's own TikTok accounts using TikTok's Login Kit and Content Posting API. Only accounts the owner owns or administers are connected, and the app does not offer sign-in to the public. The owner connects an account once through TikTok's own authorization screen on the owner's computer.

The permissions (scopes) the app asks for:

  • user.info.basic: to read the connected account's unique ID (open ID), display name and avatar, so the app can confirm the right account was connected.
  • video.upload: to send a video to the connected account's TikTok inbox as a draft, which the owner then finishes and posts in the TikTok app.

The app does not read the account's videos, followers, messages, comments or analytics, and does not post on its own without the owner's approval of each video. What it stores: the access and refresh tokens (in the operating system's keychain, never in a plain file), the account's open ID and display name, and a record of each video the owner chose to send (file name, caption, date and the publish ID TikTok returns). It is not used for advertising, is not sold, is not shared with anyone except TikTok in order to deliver the video, and is not used to train machine-learning or artificial-intelligence models. Your use of TikTok is governed by the TikTok Terms of Service, and TikTok's handling of your information by the TikTok Privacy Policy.

8. Where information is stored and how it is protected

Everything is stored on the owner's own computer. Sign-in and access tokens, and the app's Google, Meta and TikTok client credentials, are kept in the operating system's encrypted keychain, not in plain files. Channel and account details and upload records are kept in local files on that computer. The app has no server of its own, and no copy is kept in any cloud service run by the app. Communication with Google, Meta and TikTok uses encrypted connections (HTTPS).

9. Sharing

The app does not share, sell, rent or trade information with anyone. Information goes only to Google and YouTube, to Meta (Instagram and Facebook) and to TikTok, and only to carry out the uploads and account checks described above. Information would be disclosed to anyone else only if required by law.

10. Cookies and device information

The app does not place cookies, use tracking or analytics tools, or collect device identifiers. It runs on the owner's computer and talks only to Google, Meta and TikTok. This website is hosted on GitHub Pages; GitHub may log visits to it under the GitHub Privacy Statement.

11. How long information is kept

Sign-in and access tokens and channel or account details are kept while the channel or account stays connected. Upload records are kept as the owner's record of their own posts. When access is removed (see below), the stored token and channel or account details are deleted from the owner's computer within 7 days.

12. Removing access and deleting data

You can remove the app's access to your Google account at any time on the Google security settings page (Third-party apps & services). From that moment the stored token stops working and the app can no longer reach your account.

For TikTok, remove the app in the TikTok app under Settings and privacy > Security and permissions > Apps and services (Manage app permissions), after which the stored token stops working. For Instagram and Facebook, remove the app on Facebook under Settings & privacy > Settings > Apps and websites (Business Integrations), and on Instagram under Settings > Website permissions > Apps and websites; or revoke or delete the access token in the owner's Meta Business Suite. The stored token then stops working.

Data deletion requests. To ask for all data the app holds about an Instagram account, Facebook Page or YouTube channel to be deleted (its token, account details and upload records), open an issue at the contact link below, or contact the owner through the Instagram account or Page concerned, saying which account it is. It will be deleted from the owner's computer within 7 days. Posts already published on Instagram, Facebook or YouTube are not held by the app; they are removed in those services.

13. Children

The app is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect information from anyone under 13 (or the minimum age in your country).

14. Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR in the European Union, or under California law), you may have the right to ask what information is held about you, to have it corrected or deleted, and to object to its use. Use the contact link below for any such request.

15. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new effective date.

16. Contact

Questions or requests: open an issue at github.com/johnramongoedschalk-blip/ari-uploader/issues, or use the developer contact shown on the app's Google consent screen.
